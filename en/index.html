Did unemployment really fall?

Unemployment is close to the lowest levels of the PNAD Contínua survey, a series that began in 2012. Many people do not believe it and say it is a trick in the formula. Here is how IBGE counts, in simple words.

📊 See the summary in icons

The short answer

Yes, it fell. IBGE has counted the same way since 2012, under every government, and follows the rule used worldwide. Unemployment began to fall in 2021, under Bolsonaro, and kept falling under Lula.

How IBGE counts

Every three months, IBGE interviewers visit about 210,000 homes across the country and ask the same questions to everyone living there aged 14 and over.

Is it true?

False

“People who receive Bolsa Família count as employed.” IBGE does not use the benefit to decide anything. Someone who receives it and does not work, but is looking, counts as unemployed.

True

“Someone who did a 1-hour odd job counts as working.” It is the International Labour Organization's rule, used worldwide and by IBGE since 2012, under every government. That is why IBGE also publishes how many wanted to work more.

True

“People who gave up looking are not in the rate.” True, but IBGE counts them separately, and their number has also fallen.

False

“IBGE changed the formula to help Lula.” The rules have been the same since 2012. In 2025 IBGE only updated the population size with the 2022 Census and recalculated every past year, including those of previous governments.

Half true

“The drop is only an effect of the new calculation.” One columnist points out that the 2025 update helped lower the rate that quarter. But other figures, such as record numbers of people working and of formal jobs, show that employment really grew.

False

“IBGE lies about unemployment.” Bolsonaro said this in 2024. But the method is the same one used under his government, when unemployment fell from 14.9% to 7.9%.

In short

  • IBGE has counted unemployment the same way since 2012, under every government.
  • Bolsa Família is not part of the count.
  • Unemployment really fell: it began to fall under Bolsonaro, reached 5.1% at the end of 2025 and was 5.3% in June–August 2026, under Lula.
  • Many people are still in informal work or want to work more.

The old survey and the new one

Until 2016 IBGE also had the PME, which only looked at six metropolitan regions and gave lower rates. PNAD Contínua, from 2012 onwards, covers the whole country. That is why the PME's 4.3% from December 2014 should not be compared with today's numbers. On this page we only use PNAD Contínua.

Agência Pública

The 2025 update

In July 2025, IBGE began using the 2022 Census population to weight the survey responses, because the old estimate had about 4 million more people. The definitions did not change, and the whole series, since 2012, was recalculated. The chart above uses a single extraction of the current series from SIDRA table 6381, variable 4099, consulted on 10/10/2026. That way, all points share the same weighting version.
